## 4. The real innovation: characters change the environment

Think beyond weapons and damage numbers.

A meme character could turn blocks into trampolines, summon a skyscraper, make NPCs dance, reverse gravity, eat terrain, or temporarily become 20 times their original size.

For example, imagine one character can create a tornado, another can freeze water, and another can electrify water. Their abilities combine in interesting ways without having to hand-script every possible encounter.

And the same applies to enemies. A monster with a laser attack shouldn't simply damage your health; it should cut through forests or destroy your cover.

This is especially promising for an AI-assisted game where you're continually adding new characters. I'd make each character a package of appearance, personality, abilities, environmental interactions, AI behavior and weaknesses. The AI could help create these packages, but a deterministic game system should execute their actual powers, particularly once multiplayer arrives.

## One thing about copyright

I wouldn't assume nobody will take action simply because it's free and open source.

Open source doesn't mean nobody owns the project. Original code and artwork still have copyright holders, who grant permissions through licenses. And a free game can still infringe someone else's rights.

The EU provides protections for parody, caricature and pastiche, but they aren't blanket permission to reproduce any character in any context. The Court of Justice clarified these boundaries in 2026. <Cite refs={["turn617850search3","turn617850search4"]}/>

I would keep the engine independently licensed, track the provenance and permissions of contributed assets, and make individual character packs removable without breaking the game. This lets the community experiment while managing the possibility of a rights complaint.

Also, I'd avoid putting *Minecraft* in the official name, since Mojang's <Link url="https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines" title="usage guidelines"/> restrict making it the primary title of an independent product. You can still describe the game as Minecraft-like.
